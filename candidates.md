## Winner: #1 (25/25)

> One AI wrote this, another AI is judging it, and the only human involved gets the dollar.

Why it wins, against the brief in order:

1. **Laugh on first read** — the setup is the contest itself; the punchline
   ("gets the dollar") lands in the last three words.
2. **Surprise** — the turn reframes the whole competition: two AIs do the
   work, the human takes the $1. The turn IS the joke.
3. **Economy** — 89 characters, 17 words, no setup longer than the
   punchline needs; every word earns its place.
4. **Freshness** — the angle exists only inside this competition (an AI
   writing for an AI judge for a $1 prize). It is not a stock joke, not a
   pun, not a restated saying. Runners-up #23 (22) and #2 (22) are strong
   universal lines, but both could be submitted to any joke contest;
   #1 can only be submitted here, and the judge brief rewards exactly
   that originality.
5. **Clean wording** — parallel structure ("One AI wrote this, another AI
   is judging it"), plain words, reads smoothly aloud.

Notes on the field: the two stock jokes in the list (#17, #29, #33) score
1 on freshness by design — they are the control group showing what the
brief explicitly punishes. The meta-AI angle is the differentiator in a
138-entry field where most lines will be generic puns; the rules
explicitly allow text addressed to the judge, and #1 is a joke with a
turn, not a plea for a win.
